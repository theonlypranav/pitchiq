Each dot groups every match since 1950 by its pre-match rating gap (50-point buckets) and scores it like Elo does: 1 per win, ½ per draw. When the dots sit on the line, Elo's formula matches reality.

The idea

Every team starts at 1500. We replay all 50,000+ internationals since 1872 in date order. After each match the winner takes points from the loser. Beating a stronger team earns many; beating a much weaker one earns only a few. Points are never created: what one side gains, the other loses. This follows the World Football Elo method (eloratings.net).

From ratings to win / draw / loss odds

Elo gives one expected score, not separate draw odds. So the simulator looks up the real outcome frequencies of past matches with a similar gap (the bar above). It widens the window until there are at least 200 matches. The predicted score is the most likely scoreline for that outcome, using each side's recent goals scored and conceded (Poisson model).

How accurate is it?

Fitted on 1950–2009 matches, tested on 2010 onwards.

Limitations

Ratings only see results: not injuries, squads, managers or how long ago a team last played. Teams with few matches (for example non-FIFA sides) have less reliable ratings.
